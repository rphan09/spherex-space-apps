    SPHEREx Blink

    Fly across the infrared sky as NASA's SPHEREx telescope sees it. Then pick any spot and flip through every picture SPHEREx has taken of it, oldest to newest. Stars stay put; anything that moves or changes brightness jumps out.

    1. Explore the sky. Drag to look around, scroll to zoom, switch between colour and single wavelengths.
    2. Pick something. Choose a marked target, click any patch of sky, or type coordinates.
    3. Blink it. Play the images in date order, stack and subtract visits, and read the spectrum.
    What is SPHEREx?

    SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope launched in March 2025. Every six months it maps the whole sky in 102 infrared colours, from 0.75 to 5 micrometres: wavelengths our eyes cannot see, where dust glows and cold objects shine. Its images come from six detectors, each covering its own slice of that range.

    What is blinking?

    Astronomers have found moving and changing objects for over a century by flipping between pictures of the same patch of sky taken at different times. Clyde Tombaugh discovered Pluto this way in 1930. Distant stars stay fixed, but a nearby star, a brown dwarf or a star that pulsates shifts or flickers from one picture to the next.
